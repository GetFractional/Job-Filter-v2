Primary user and journey truth

Primary user

A skeptical, overloaded applicant who wants reusable leverage, clear reasoning, and calm operating surfaces. They dislike hype, black-box scoring, and admin sprawl.

Core belief shift

The product is not here to “write faster.” It is here to help the user decide better and reuse verified work across serious applications.

Stage Dominant user question Dominant move
D1 Add your experience Is this setup worth doing? Start from one trusted source and understand why the work compounds.
D2 Check your history Are the facts right enough to trust? Edit the profile, resolve conflicts, and approve reusable proof inline.
D4 Role Discovery Which role direction is strongest now? Compare roles in a matrix and activate one role lane.
D5 Feed Which new jobs are worth saving? Search first, quick-evaluate, and save intentionally.
D6 Saved Which saved job deserves a real decision next? Use the queue, filters, and quick inspector to pick the next review.
D7 Review Should I spend real effort on this job? Review fit, proof, disqualifiers, and asset direction before promotion.
D8 Applications What active work needs attention today? Manage stage, next action, due date, and lightweight context calmly.
D9 Workspace How do I do the work without losing context? Edit assets, keep notes and messages nearby, and complete the next action.

Global interaction grammar

Field grammar

Use type-aware controls only. Searchable multi-selects for skills and tools. Date pickers for timelines. Numeric steppers or masked inputs for compensation and counts. Repeatable row editors for responsibilities, outcomes, stories, contacts, and messages.

Validation and guidance

Keep validation inline. Tooltips and short helpers belong next to the relevant control. Do not use helper rails, badge clutter, or long explainer paragraphs inside the product.

Add behavior

Add role, add-row, and add-record actions live inside the owning table, inspector, or section header. They do not live as ornamental page-header actions.

Surface grammar

Tables for canonical records and role matrices. Boards only when stage scan matters. Drawers for quick review. Modals for one bounded edit or confirmation only.

Role lane, score, and disqualifier model

Lane model

One active role lane now. Role grouping remains visible and explainable. Multi-lane value can be hinted later, but it must not become a required first-slice interaction model.

Role comparison pattern

D4 must use a real matrix with visible columns for role, recommendation, Role fit, Lane opportunity, proof coverage, scope match, tools or domain match, comp or remote alignment, and disqualifiers.

Measure Rule
Role fit Visible only with factor-level explanation. No opaque composite score.
Job fit Visible in Saved, Review, and compactly downstream, always with strengths, gaps, and proof usage context.
Lane opportunity Visible and separate from Role fit. It is not a side-sheet gimmick.
Priority Operational sorting helper only. Never the only explanation.
Disqualifier class Meaning Examples Surface behavior
hard Likely dealbreaker now remote mismatch, comp mismatch, missing required skill or tool Filterable, overrideable, and blocks promotion by default until overridden.
soft Meaningful concern but not a blocker limited industry match, weaker tenure signal Stays visible in rows, inspectors, and review blocks.
verify Missing or uncertain information unclear remote policy, missing compensation detail, fuzzy seniority requirement Prompt the user to confirm or temporarily override with rationale.

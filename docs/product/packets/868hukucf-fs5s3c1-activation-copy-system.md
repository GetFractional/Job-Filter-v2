## 2. Copy principles

### Belief shift the interface should create

The user should move from:

- "This looks like more setup."

to:

- "This is the clean foundation that will make the rest of my search easier to run."

### Friction to remove

The copy system must remove:

- source-choice hesitation at the first screen
- fear that the product is reasoning from bad imports
- fear that proof or fit is a black box
- fear that drafts will overstate what the user can defend
- vague stage language that sounds like internal workflow labels

### What clarity means here

Clarity means the user can answer these questions within a few seconds:

1. What do I do next?
2. Why is this step worth doing?
3. What carries forward if I do it well?
4. What is being reviewed, approved, or left out?

### What trust means here

Trust means:

- the app says what it is doing in plain language
- the app does not sound smarter than the evidence
- the user can inspect fit, proof, provenance, and exclusions
- the app never implies send, apply, or automation beyond what the product actually supports

### What motivation means here

Motivation here is not hype. It is calm momentum.

The copy should help the user feel:

- this is worth finishing
- this step has a concrete payoff
- I am still in control
- the app is helping me make better decisions, not just produce more output

## 3. Voice and tone rules for activation

### Tone attributes

- calm
- precise
- grounded
- quietly encouraging
- adult
- non-theatrical

### Sentence-style rules

- prefer one clear thought per sentence
- use active voice
- name the object the user is working on
- keep helper text tight
- avoid stacked abstractions
- avoid cleverness where a direct noun or verb will do

### Default tense and person

- present tense
- second person when speaking to the user
- first person only in direct CTA language where natural, such as `Build my job profile`

### Rules for buttons

- buttons must name the action, not the system state
- use short verb-led labels
- do not use vague terms like `Run`, `Generate`, `Proceed`, or `Optimize`
- do not use internal workflow labels as button copy

### Rules for helper text

- helper text explains one decision, one boundary, or one consequence
- helper text must not narrate the product to itself
- helper text must not sound like release notes or implementation notes

### Rules for labels

- labels should use user-native nouns
- labels should be literal before they are elegant
- labels should not force the user to learn internal vocabulary just to proceed

### Rules for boundaries

- boundary copy must say what will not happen automatically
- boundary copy must say what still needs user review or approval
- boundary copy must stay matter-of-fact, not dramatic

### Good product copy in this app sounds like

- `Turn your experience into a search system you can trust.`
- `Check your history.`
- `See what supports this job.`
- `You review the facts before they shape fit, proof, or job decisions.`

### Bad product copy in this app sounds like

- `Normalize your profile intelligence.`
- `Activation complete.`
- `Run job-fit analysis.`
- `Signed-in desktop.`
- `Accepted inputs.`

### Conflict resolution from Chapter 02

`Continue setup` remains an approved signed-in progress phrase from Chapter 02, but it is not allowed to replace more specific state-exit actions when the user is approving truth, creating a lane, or confirming a job review.

Use:

- `Continue setup` for route-level momentum controls

Use specific actions for decisive state exits:

- `Confirm history`
- `Continue to Role Discovery`
- `Create role lane`
- `Start application`

## 15. Tooltip, drawer, modal, and helper-copy rules

| Surface | What it is for | Length rule | Tone rule | What not to do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| tooltip | define a short label, status, or model name | one short sentence, ideally under 10 words | literal and neutral | no persuasion, no mini tutorials |
| drawer | explain a decision model or inspect supporting detail without losing page context | title up to 4 words, intro up to 20 words | calm and specific | no long preambles, no marketing voice |
| side sheet | hold a structured secondary task such as job capture or lane opportunity | title up to 4 words, support line up to 22 words | task-oriented | no duplicated page narration |
| modal | confirm a destructive or high-risk change | title up to 4 words, body up to 18 words | consequence first, action second | no `Are you sure?`, no vague confirm buttons |
| guidance card | explain one payoff, boundary, or next-step rule | title up to 4 words, body up to 22 words | steady and helpful | no tours, no hype, no self-congratulation |
| inline issue state | flag one fix, gap, or unresolved detail | label up to 3 words, helper up to 12 words | direct and practical | no error theater, no technical jargon |

### Cross-surface rules

- helper copy must stay subordinate to the dominant action
- drawers and sheets may explain logic, but they must not re-sell the entire product
- modals must name the consequence in plain language
- tooltips must never carry trust-critical language that the user could miss

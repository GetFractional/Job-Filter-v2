# Copy And Naming Decision Pack

Last updated: 2026-04-23

## Naming Status

Do not start final naming until the following are stable:

- product thesis
- customer promise
- category wedge
- free-tier value
- first launch package

The current working name remains `Job Filter` until a later naming pass replaces it.

## Product Anchor

Primary:

`Apply with confidence to roles you can prove you fit.`

Brand frame:

`Pursue the best opportunities for your career with clarity, proof, and control.`

Secondary:

`Build a reusable profile from your real experience, see which opportunities are worth your time, and review every claim before it moves into an application.`

## Positioning

For people trying to build a better career through better opportunity decisions, Job Filter helps you choose better-fit roles, prove your fit, and prepare reviewed application materials without unsupported claims.

Unlike resume builders, job trackers, and auto-apply tools, Job Filter connects your reusable Profile, job requirements, source evidence, and application work in one reviewable system.

The early wedge remains users who care deeply about fit quality, trust, and reusable proof, including broad-history professionals, focused climbers, switchers, returners, and signal-builders.

## Audience Handling Rule

Public-facing copy should speak broadly to better opportunity decisions and career progress. Product proof, examples, and onboarding should still show the wedge cases that prove why the system is different.

## Copy Rules

- Speak to the person, not the category.
- Prefer concrete verbs: build, verify, review, decide, apply.
- Name the mechanism close to the claim.
- Keep proof and trust boundaries visible.
- Use AI as a helper, not the hero.
- Use `Free tier` in governance and product-plan contexts.
- Use `free to start` only as public-facing clarity when useful.

## Disallowed Language

- job-search operating system
- dream job faster
- 10x your job search
- turbocharge
- ATS hack
- recruiter-ready in seconds
- apply on autopilot
- done-for-you applications
- guaranteed interviews

- effortless
- magic

## Naming Criteria

A strong new name should:

- speak to confidence, fit, proof, or direction
- feel calm and credible for experienced professionals
- still make sense to a motivated early-career or thinner-history user
- avoid sounding like an auto-apply or hustle tool
- avoid sounding like a generic resume builder
- be easy to say
- have a plausible domain path
- work in product UI as a noun and brand
- leave room for future profile, jobs, applications, and workspace features

## Naming Themes To Explore Later

| Theme | Rationale | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Proof and evidence | Closest to differentiation. | Can sound legalistic or cold. |
| Direction and fit | Speaks to role choice. | Can sound like career coaching. |
| Confidence and readiness | Speaks to emotional outcome. | Can become generic if not grounded. |
| Signal and clarity | Speaks to filtering noise. | Can sound technical or abstract. |
| Narrative and story | Speaks to broad-history users. | Can over-index on writing. |

## Naming Process

1. Lock Product OS package.
2. Extract customer language from PRD, teardown, and beta conversations.
3. Generate 50-100 candidates across naming themes.
4. Screen for disallowed associations and category confusion.
5. Score against naming rubric.
6. Shortlist 8-12 names.
7. Run domain and trademark sanity checks.
8. Test top names against homepage hero and app nav.
9. Pick one primary and one fallback.

## Naming Rubric

Score 1-5:

- clarity for target user
- fit with `apply with confidence`
- differentiation from Teal/Huntr/Rezi/Simplify
- credibility for experienced professionals
- product extensibility
- memorability
- domain viability
- low cringe risk

## Copy QA Checklist

- Does the copy say who is helped?
- Does it say what changes for them?
- Does it reveal the mechanism?
- Does it avoid unsupported outcome claims?
- Does it preserve review-before-send?
- Does it avoid auto-apply implications?
- Does it sound like a real person would say it?
